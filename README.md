# Solver-Aware Simulation of Drip-Irrigation Wetting Bulbs in Unsaturated Soils

## Reproducibility archive

This Zenodo archive contains the computational notebooks, processed datasets, simulation outputs, and figure-generation files associated with the study **“Solver-Aware Simulation of Drip-Irrigation Wetting Bulbs in Unsaturated Soils.”**

### Created: Gregory Guevara 
### September 2026

The archive is intended to provide transparent traceability from the numerical model to the reported diagnostics, sensitivity analyses, wetting-bulb geometries, and publication figures.

The workflow is not intended as a general-purpose hydrological modeling package. It reproduces the numerical framework and analyses used in the associated study.

---

## Main notebooks

### `solver_Richard_Eq.ipynb`

This notebook contains the numerical model and simulation workflow.

Main components include:

- two-dimensional axisymmetric Richards-equation formulation;
- van Genuchten–Mualem hydraulic functions;
- conservative finite-volume spatial discretization;
- implicit backward-Euler time integration;
- modified Picard nonlinear iteration;
- harmonic averaging of intercell hydraulic conductivity;
- texture-specific initial conditions and numerical controls;
- localized prescribed-flux emitter boundary;
- no-flow radial boundary and symmetry axis;
- unit-gradient free-drainage lower boundary;
- solver rescue logic and convergence diagnostics;
- reference-state screening;
- discharge and irrigation-time simulations;
- hydraulic-parameter sensitivity;
- numerical-control sensitivity;
- numerical consistency checks;
- export of simulation summaries and archived numerical states.

### `Figures_Analysis.ipynb`

This notebook contains the post-processing, analysis, and figure-generation workflow.

It reproduces:

- **Figure 2** — UNSODA-based soil hydraulic variability;
- **Figure 4** — solver-aware reference-state selection;
- **Figure 5** — representative wetting-bulb geometries;
- **Figure 6** — discharge × irrigation-time response for Loam;
- **Figure 7** — hydraulic-parameter sensitivity;
- **Figure 8** — numerical-control sensitivity;
- **Figure 9** — computational runtime response;
- supplementary simulation-definition sensitivity.

**Figures 1 and 3 are conceptual schematics and are not generated by the numerical notebooks.**

## Numerical framework

The model solves the Richards equation in a two-dimensional axisymmetric domain representing localized drip irrigation.

The principal numerical features are:

- finite-volume discretization in cylindrical coordinates;
- backward-Euler time integration;
- modified Picard linearization;
- harmonic averaging of hydraulic conductivity;
- van Genuchten–Mualem hydraulic properties;
- prescribed surface flux over a finite emitter radius;
- no-flow outer radial boundary;
- axisymmetric boundary at the centerline;
- unit-gradient free drainage at the lower boundary.

The computational domain used for the main simulations is 50 cm in depth × 50 cm in radius with a 1 cm × 1 cm grid.

The principal simulated textures are:

- Loamy Sand;
- Loam;
- Clay Loam.

---

## Interpretation and scope

The archived simulations are numerical reference scenarios rather than site-specific experimental calibrations.

Important interpretation notes:

- initial pressure head is texture specific;
- cross-texture comparisons therefore reflect both hydraulic parameterization and texture-specific initial water status;
- the surface emitter is represented as a finite-radius prescribed-flux source;
- positive-pressure ponding and dynamic expansion of the wetted surface area are not explicitly modeled;
- the storage ratio is used as a comparative applied-water storage-retention diagnostic and is not a complete mass-balance residual;
- non-convergent time steps are retained and reported as part of the solver-aware analysis;
- runtime values are implementation- and hardware-dependent and should be interpreted comparatively;
- numerical consistency checks support the reported comparisons but do not constitute a formal grid- and time-step-convergence study.

Ensure that the archived input CSV, PKL, and NPZ files are present in the expected relative locations.

Open:

```text
Figures_Analysis.ipynb
```

and execute the desired figure blocks or run the notebook sequentially.

Generated figure files are written to:

```text

```

## Reproducibility safeguards

To preserve consistency with the published study:

1. Do not mix CSV, PKL, or NPZ outputs generated by different solver versions.
2. Keep the archived filenames unchanged unless the notebooks and documentation are updated together.
3. Run the notebooks from the archive root so that relative paths resolve correctly.
4. Use the archived processed files to reproduce the reported figures without rerunning exploratory simulations.
5. If simulations are rerun, document any software-version or hardware differences before comparing runtime values.
6. Treat flagged runtime observations as diagnostic observations, not as deleted or corrected data.
